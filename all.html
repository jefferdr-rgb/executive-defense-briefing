2. Architecture & The Why
Technical Architecture & Design Rationale

How the System Is Built & Why

A clear breakdown of the 6 core components and the practical reason behind each design decision.
Design Principle: We built this system in pure Go to run entirely on a standard office computer. We avoided public cloud AI, avoided Python dependency bloat, and put strict mathematical gates between every step so the software cannot hallucinate, skip rules, or miscalculate pricing.
STEP 01 The Document Reader (Intake & Shredder)
What It Does
Automatically pulls solicitations (RFPs) from government websites and shreds Section C (Work Statement), Section L (Submission Instructions), and Section M (Evaluation Criteria) into a structured checklist of individual requirements.
Why We Built It This Way
Government RFPs are 200+ pages of dense legal text. Human capture teams spend 3 to 5 days just manually building an Excel compliance matrix. The software does it in 60 seconds with zero missed "shall" clauses.
STEP 02 The Compliance Lock (Gate 1)
What It Does
Matches every single government requirement directly to a designated section in the proposal outline. Writing is physically blocked until 100% of requirements have an assigned home.
Why We Built It This Way
Missing a single minor requirement (like a safety certificate or ISO clause) causes automatic disqualification by government evaluators. This lock guarantees zero dropped requirements before writing a single sentence.
STEP 03 The Company Memory Vault (Past Performance Matcher)
What It Does
Instantly searches the company's past contracts, won proposals, employee resumes, and Deltek labor categories in under 2 milliseconds on a local database.
Why We Built It This Way
Public AI tools make up fake qualifications when they don't know the answer. By grounding the system in an air-gapped local database of actual past performance, every technical claim is backed by real corporate evidence.
STEP 04 The Drafting Engine with Instant Safety Switch
What It Does
Drafts technical proposal narrative at high speed using local models, while an internal monitor watches every word as it is generated. If an unsupported claim appears, it cuts the generation in under 50 milliseconds.
Why We Built It This Way
Catching AI mistakes after writing a 30-page document wastes hours of human editing. Stopping the draft the instant it drifts prevents bad assumptions from infecting the rest of the proposal.
STEP 05 The Penny Reconciler & Compliance Audit (Gate 2 & 3)
What It Does
Extracts every headcount, labor category, and pricing claim from the written narrative and compares them against the pricing spreadsheet. Then, it runs an automated mock evaluation using government scoring rubrics.
Why We Built It This Way
In typical proposals, the technical team writes "we provide 4 engineers" while the pricing team budgeted for 3, causing immediate audit penalties. This step enforces exact $0.00 mathematical agreement before humans review.
STEP 06 The Word Document Generator (.docx)
What It Does
Compiles the proposal directly into a clean, formatted Microsoft Word document (.docx) with compliant multi-column tables, shaded callout boxes, headers, footers, and government CUI distribution markings.
Why We Built It This Way
Standard AI software produces Markdown text, which breaks completely when converted to Word—tables scramble and page margins fail. Writing directly into Word OpenXML ensures the output looks like a professional $100M defense bid.

Why This Architecture Wins in Defense

Most AI startups build cloud web apps that fail government security rules and hallucinate compliance facts. By building a pure Go, on-premise system with rigid mathematical checks between each step, we created a tool that defense executives can actually trust with their most critical bids.

3. Node Isolation
Hardware & Network Architecture

Why Separate Nodes? Why Not One Big Box?

How isolating each function onto dedicated nodes protects company data and guarantees proposal compliance.

The Obvious Question: "Why not just buy one powerful computer and run everything on it?"

In software, putting everything into one box sounds simpler. But in defense contracting, mixing public internet scraping, classified company rates, and AI text generation inside the same computer is a catastrophic security and operational failure. Here is why the system is broken into isolated, dedicated nodes.

The Three Reasons One Big Box Fails

1. Security & The One-Way Firebreak

The system has to talk to public websites (SAM.gov) to download solicitations, but it also handles sensitive company data (Deltek labor rates, salaries, and executive resumes). If one computer does both, a software bug or external attack could leak company rates onto the public web. Separating them creates a physical firebreak: public documents come in, but company data can never leak out.

2. Independent Quality Auditing

When one AI model writes a proposal and audits its own work, it suffers from confirmation bias—it ignores its own mistakes. By placing the audit function on an isolated node that has no involvement in the drafting, the auditor acts like a skeptical government evaluator with zero pride in the writing.

3. GPU Resource Starvation

Generating technical text requires 100% of a GPU's compute power. If document parsing, database searches, and cost audits run on that same GPU, the entire system freezes and response times slow to a crawl. Isolating tasks keeps the system fast and responsive.

4. CMMC Level 2 Compliance

Federal cybersecurity rules require defense contractors to isolate Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI). By putting sensitive company data inside an isolated private enclave with zero internet connection, the system meets strict defense security mandates out of the box.

What Each Isolated Node Does & Why It Stays Separate
Node 1: The Public Airlock
Public Facing (External)
What It Does
Reaches out to SAM.gov and public agency portals to download RFP solicitations, amendments, and competitor intelligence.
Why It Must Be Isolated
It is the only part of the system connected to the outside internet. It contains zero company data and has no access to internal financial records.
Node 2: The Company Data Vault
Air-Gapped (Zero Internet)
What It Does
Stores corporate past performance, winning proposal excerpts, employee resumes, and Deltek Costpoint labor categories.
Why It Must Be Isolated
Protects the company's "secret sauce." This node has zero internet access and only accepts read requests through strict internal filters.
Node 3: The Compliance & Pricing Engine
Internal Private Network
What It Does
Builds the compliance matrix, allocates page limits, and manages the cost volume numbers (rates, hours, FTEs).
Why It Must Be Isolated
Enforces mathematical ground truth. It acts as the traffic cop between the drafting team and the pricing team, preventing human drift.
Node 4: The Drafting Engine
Dedicated GPU Compute
What It Does
Runs high-speed local AI models to turn requirements and past performance into compliant proposal narrative.
Why It Must Be Isolated
Isolating GPU compute prevents memory crashes. It receives instructions in clean batches and forgets them immediately after drafting, preventing data leakage between bids.
Node 5: The Adversarial Reviewer
Independent Auditor
What It Does
Simulates a hostile government Source Selection Evaluation Board (SSEB). Checks written text for unverified claims, weak language, and pricing mismatches.
Why It Must Be Isolated
True auditing requires complete separation. If the drafting node evaluated itself, it would rubber-stamp its own errors. Node 5 evaluates independently with zero bias.
Node 6: The Executive Control Glass
Human-in-the-Loop
What It Does
The visual dashboard where capture managers see real-time progress, review compliance scores, and give final approval.
Why It Must Be Isolated
Keeps humans firmly in control. The autonomous engine cannot finalize or submit any document without an explicit human sign-off on this screen.

The Takeaway for an Advisor

One big box is how consumer software is built. Isolated nodes are how military and defense systems are built. This architecture ensures that public internet data never touches private pricing, AI models never grade their own homework, and the company's proprietary data never leaves the building.
